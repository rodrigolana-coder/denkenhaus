Earn × Win

You earn money from work.

I earn £2,000 a month.

She earns a good salary.

You win money in a competition, game or lottery.

He won £500 in a competition.

I never play the lottery, so I never win.

Borrow × Lend

Borrow = receive temporarily.

Can I borrow £20? → Posso pegar £20 emprestado?

Lend = give temporarily.

Can you lend me £20? → Você pode me emprestar £20?

Buy × Sell

Buy: you give money and receive something.

I buy clothes online.

She buys a coffee every morning.

Sell: you give something and receive money.

I sell old books online.

They sell vegetables at the market.

How to... talk about money

A: Do you save money every month? — B: Yes, I usually do.

A: What do you spend your money on? — B: I spend a lot of money on food.

A: Do you ever buy things online? — B: Yes, sometimes.

A: Can I borrow some money? — B: Sure.

A: Can you lend me £10? — B: Yes, of course.

Grammar Review — Present Simple

1. Affirmative

We use the Present Simple to talk about regular money habits.

I save money every month.

You spend a lot on food.

We pay our bills online.

They buy things on the Internet.

He saves money every month.

She earns a good salary.

He spends too much money.

She buys clothes online.

2. Negative — DON'T / DOESN'T

I don't borrow money.

We don't invest online.

They don't buy expensive clothes.

She doesn't spend much money.

He doesn't save enough.

My brother doesn't play the lottery.

3. Questions

Do you save money?

Do you buy things online?

Do they invest money?

Does she earn a lot?

Does he spend a lot of money?

4. Adverbs of frequency — review

We can use frequency adverbs to describe our money habits.

always · usually · often · sometimes · rarely · never

I always pay my bills on time.

I usually save some money every month.

She often buys clothes online.

We sometimes eat at expensive restaurants.

He rarely borrows money.

I never play the lottery.

5. Useful money expressions

earn £600 a month · save money · spend money

pay a bill · pay for something · invest money

buy something online · sell something online · win money

6. Prepositions with money verbs

SPEND + ON: I spend a lot of money on food.

PAY + FOR: I pay for my coffee.

BORROW + FROM: I borrow money from my parents.

LEND + TO: I lend money to my brother.
