Dialogue 1 — My First Job
EmmaDid you have a job when you were a teenager?
LucasYes. I worked part-time in a cinema.
EmmaReally? What did you do there?
LucasI cleaned the cinema and helped customers.
EmmaDid you like it?
LucasYes! I loved that job.
EmmaWhat did you do with the money?
LucasI saved most of it.

Dialogue 3 — A Part-Time Job
SophieWhat was your first job?
DanielI worked in a supermarket.
SophieWhat did you do?
DanielI stacked shelves and sometimes cleaned the shop.
SophieDid you work every day?
DanielNo. I worked after school and on Saturdays.
SophieDid you enjoy it?
DanielYes. I liked earning my own money.

Grammar — Past Simple: Regular Verbs

1. Affirmative

We use the Past Simple to talk about completed actions in the past. With regular verbs, we normally add -ed.

Subject + verb-ed:

I worked in a cinema.

You cleaned the house.

He washed cars.

She delivered newspapers.

We started work at eight.

They lived in London.

The verb is the same for all persons.

2. Regular verbs — spelling

Most verbs: + ED

work → worked · clean → cleaned · wash → washed · help → helped · start → started · listen → listened · need → needed

Verbs ending in -E: + D

live → lived · love → loved · hate → hated

Consonant + Y → IED

tidy → tidied · study → studied

I tidied my bedroom yesterday.

3. Present × Past

Present: I work in a cinema. → Past: I worked in a cinema.

Present: She cleans the house. → Past: She cleaned the house.

Present: They wash cars. → Past: They washed cars.

Present: We live in London. → Past: We lived in London.

4. Past time expressions — review

yesterday · yesterday morning · last night · last weekend · last week · last month · last year · two days ago · five years ago · when I was a teenager

I worked last Saturday.

She cleaned the house yesterday.

They lived there five years ago.

5. Pronunciation — -ed

The -ed ending has three common pronunciations.

/t/ after voiceless sounds: worked, washed, helped, liked, finished

/d/ after many voiced sounds: cleaned, lived, loved, listened

/ɪd/ after verbs ending in /t/ or /d/ sounds: started, needed, hated

We don't normally pronounce -ed as an extra syllable except with the /ɪd/ sound.
