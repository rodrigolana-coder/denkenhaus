Dialogue — Learning a Language
SophieYou speak Spanish really well. How long have you been learning it?
DanielAbout two years, but speaking is still the hardest part for me.
SophieReally? I think you’re quite good at speaking.
DanielThanks, but I’m much better at reading and understanding people.
SophieWhat are you not very good at?
DanielI’m terrible at remembering new vocabulary. I learn a word and then forget it a few days later.
SophieI have the opposite problem. I’m good at remembering words, but I find pronunciation difficult.
DanielHow do you practise it?
SophieI listen to podcasts and repeat sentences aloud. It really helps.
DanielThat’s a good idea. I should probably practise speaking more often.
SophieDefinitely. The important thing is not to be afraid of making mistakes.
DanielThat’s true. Sometimes I know what I want to say, but I worry about saying it incorrectly.
SophieEveryone makes mistakes when they’re learning. You just have to keep practising.
DanielYou’re right. Maybe I’ll start speaking Spanish with you from now on!

Be good at + noun

We use be good at to talk about abilities and skills.

I’m good at languages.
Eu sou bom em línguas.

She’s good at maths.
Ela é boa em matemática.

Be good at + verb-ing

When we use a verb after at, we use the -ing form.

I’m good at speaking English.
She’s good at remembering names.
They’re good at solving problems.

Talking about difficulties

I’m not very good at cooking.
I’m bad at remembering numbers.
I’m terrible at drawing.

6. Eu acho pronúncia bastante difícil.

7. Minha maior dificuldade é entender as pessoas quando elas falam rápido.
My biggest difficulty is understanding people when they speak quickly.
8. Eu sou melhor em leitura do que em fala.
I’m better at reading than speaking.
9. Como você pratica sua pronúncia?
How do you practise your pronunciation?

I try to repeat the sentences aloud.
11. Não tenha medo de cometer erros.

13. Qual é o seu maior ponto forte em inglês?
