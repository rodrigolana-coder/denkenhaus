How to... talk about dates

A: When is the concert? — B: It’s on June ninth.

A: When is your birthday? — B: It’s on August twenty-first.

A: What time does the show start? — B: It starts at seven o’clock.

A: Is there a performance on Friday? — B: Yes, there is.

Section 2 of 5
Grammar

1. IN

Use in with months, years, and parts of the day.

in January, in March, in October

in 2026, in 1995

in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening

My birthday is in August.

I work in the morning.

2. ON

Use on with days and dates.

on Monday, on Friday, on Saturday

on June 9th, on March 16th, on October 31st

The concert is on Friday.

My birthday is on July 12th.

3. AT

Use at with times.

at 7:00, at 8:30, at noon, at midnight

The concert starts at eight o’clock.

We have lunch at noon.

4. IN × ON × AT

UsePrepositionExample
monthinin June
yearinin 2026
morning / afternoon / eveninginin the evening
dayonon Monday
dateonon March 5th
timeatat 7:30

5. Saying dates

British English: 9 June → the ninth of June

American English: June 9 → June ninth

Both forms are correct.

My birthday is on the twenty-first of August. / My birthday is on August twenty-first.
