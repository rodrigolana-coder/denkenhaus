How to... talk about people in the past

A: Who was he? — B: He was a famous scientist.

A: What was her job? — B: She was an actress.

A: Where was he born? — B: He was born in Germany.

A: When was she born? — B: She was born in 1929.

A: Were they famous? — B: Yes, they were.

Section 2 of 5
Grammar

1. Past Simple of TO BE

In the present we use am / is / are. In the past we use was / were.

SubjectPresentPast
Iamwas
youarewere
heiswas
sheiswas
itiswas
wearewere
theyarewere

2. WAS / WERE

WAS — with I / he / she / it

I was at home. He was a musician. She was a famous actress. It was a great film.

WERE — with you / we / they

You were very young. We were at school. They were famous singers.

3. Negative

WAS NOT → WASN’T

I wasn’t at home. He wasn’t a doctor. She wasn’t American.

WERE NOT → WEREN’T

You weren’t at school. We weren’t tired. They weren’t actors.

4. Questions

With was/were, put the verb before the subject.

Was he a singer? Was she famous? Was it a good film?

Were you at school? Were they friends? Were your parents there?

Short answers

Was he famous? — Yes, he was. / No, he wasn’t.

Were they musicians? — Yes, they were. / No, they weren’t.

5. Present × Past

I am a teacher now. I was a student in 2010.

She is in London now. She was in Paris yesterday.

They are adults now. They were children in 2005.

6. WAS/WERE + BORN

We use was/were born to talk about someone’s birth.

I was born in 1995. She was born in London. He was born in 1980.

They were born in Brazil. My brothers were born in Curitiba.

Where were you born? — I was born in Brazil.

When was she born? — She was born in 1998.
