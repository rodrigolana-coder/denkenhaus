How to... talk about abilities

A: Can you cook? — B: Yes, I can.

A: Can you swim? — B: No, I can’t.

A: Can your sister speak French? — B: Yes, she can.

A: Can your father drive? — B: Yes, he can.

Section 2 of 5
Grammar

1. CAN — Ability

We use can to talk about abilities.

I can cook.

You can swim.

He can drive.

She can speak French.

We can dance.

They can play football.

2. CAN’T — Inability

Use can’t to say that someone is not able to do something.

I can’t sing.

She can’t drive.

He can’t play the piano.

They can’t speak Japanese.

3. Questions with CAN

Can you cook?

Can she dance?

Can he drive?

Can they swim?

Can your brother play chess?

Short answers

Yes, I can. / No, I can’t.

Yes, she can. / No, she can’t.

4. Word order

Affirmative — Subject + can + verb

I can swim. She can cook.

Negative — Subject + can’t + verb

He can’t drive. They can’t sing.

Question — Can + subject + verb?

Can you dance? Can she play tennis?

5. Review — Present Simple × CAN

Present Simple

I work in a hospital. She studies English. They play football every Saturday.

Ability

I can cook. She can speak French. They can swim.
