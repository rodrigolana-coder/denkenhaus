Dialogue 1 — A Bad Day
EmmaYou look upset. Are you OK?
LucasNot really. I feel terrible today.
EmmaWhy? What happened?
LucasI got some bad news this morning.
EmmaI'm sorry. Are you going to stay at home tonight?
LucasYes, I am. I'm not going to go out.
EmmaAre you going to watch a film?
LucasMaybe. I think I'm going to relax and go to bed early.

JohnYes, I am. I'm going to read about the company and write some notes.
AnnaAre you going to stay up late?
JohnNo, I'm not. I'm going to sleep early.

Dialogue 3 — A New Beginning
SophieYou look really excited!
DanielI am! I'm going to start university next month.
SophieThat's great! Are you going to move to another city?
DanielYes, I am.
SophieAren't you going to miss your family?
DanielOf course, but I'm also happy about the change.
SophieWere you nervous when you first decided to go?
DanielYes. I felt very nervous at first, but then I became excited about it.

How to talk about emotions

A: How do you feel?
B: I feel nervous.

A: Why are you upset?
B: Because I got some bad news.

A: Are you scared?
B: A little.

A: Are you excited about your trip?
B: Yes, I am!

Grammar

Going to — Negative

Use not going to + verb when something is not part of a future plan.

I: I'm not going to work tomorrow.
He / She / It: She isn't going to stay at home.
You / We / They: They aren't going to move house.

Going to — Questions

Put am / is / are before the subject.

Am I going to...?
Is he / she going to...?
Are you / we / they going to...?

Are you going to study tonight?
Is she going to buy a new car?
Are they going to travel next month?
Is he going to start university?

Short answers

Feel → felt

Present: I feel nervous today.
Past: I felt nervous yesterday.
I felt tired after work.
She felt happy when she got the news.

Become → became

Present: He wants to become a doctor.
Past: He became a doctor in 2020.
She became nervous.
The situation became difficult.

Begin → began

Present: Classes begin at nine.
Past: The class began at nine yesterday.
The film began at eight.
My new job began last Monday.

Write → wrote

Present: I write emails every day.
Past: I wrote an email yesterday.
She wrote me a message.
He wrote some notes before the interview.

Read → read

The spelling does not change, but the pronunciation does.

Present: read /riːd/ — I read every night.
Past: read /red/ — I read that book last year.

Did + base verb — Review

I felt nervous. → Did you feel nervous? → I didn't feel nervous.
She wrote an email. → Did she write an email? → She didn't write an email.
He became angry. → Did he become angry?

Never use: ❌ Did you felt nervous?
