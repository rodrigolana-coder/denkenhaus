Why Is the Bald Eagle the Symbol of the United States?
The bald eagle is one of the most famous symbols of the United States.
It became the country's national symbol in 1782 because it represents strength, freedom and courage.
Bald eagles live only in North America.
They usually build very large nests at the top of tall trees or on cliffs near rivers and lakes.
They are excellent hunters and can see fish from a very long distance.
They often fly high in the sky before catching their food.
Today, the bald eagle appears on many official American documents, passports, coins and government buildings.
Years ago, bald eagles were in danger because of hunting and pollution.
Today, thanks to conservation programs, their population has grown again, and they are no longer considered endangered in the United States.
If you visit places like Alaska, Florida or the Yellowstone National Park, you might be lucky enough to see one in the wild.
