Dialogue 2 — A Special Restaurant
LucasThis restaurant is very old.
AnnaReally? When did it open?
LucasIn 1952.
AnnaWow! How do you say that in English?
LucasNineteen fifty-two.
AnnaThat’s easy! Now, I’m hungry. What would you like to eat?
LucasI’d like the chicken with rice.
AnnaMe too. And I’d like some chocolate cake for dessert.
Dialogue 3 — Birthday Dinner
SophieMy birthday is next Saturday.
DanielReally? What date is it?
SophieIt’s on September twenty-first.

SophieNo. I’m having dinner at my favourite restaurant.
DanielWhat would you like to eat?

DanielAnd chocolate cake?
SophieOf course! I love it!

Section 2 of 5
Grammar Review

1. LIKE × WOULD LIKE

❤️ LIKE — general preferences

I like chocolate. She likes seafood. We like eating at restaurants. He doesn’t like fish.

🍽️ WOULD LIKE — something you want

I’d like some chocolate. I’d like the chicken, please. We’d like two coffees.

Question: Would you like + noun?

Would you like some water? Would you like a dessert?

Answers: Yes, please. / No, thank you.

2. CAN I HAVE...?

Use Can I have...? to order or ask for something.

Can I have the menu, please? Can I have the fish? Can I have some water? Can I have the bill, please?

3. Saying years

1900–1999 — divide the year into two parts

1935 → nineteen thirty-five, 1952 → nineteen fifty-two, 1969 → nineteen sixty-nine, 1987 → nineteen eighty-seven, 1999 → nineteen ninety-nine

2000–2009

2000 → two thousand, 2001 → two thousand and one, 2006 → two thousand and six

2010 onwards

2010 → twenty ten, 2015 → twenty fifteen, 2020 → twenty twenty, 2026 → twenty twenty-six

4. IN / ON / AT

IN — months and years

in September, in 1952, in 1998, in 2026

ON — days and dates

on Saturday, on September 21st, on June 5th

AT — times

at 7:00, at 8:30, at noon, at midnight

Compare: The restaurant opened in 1952. My birthday is on September 21st. Dinner is at 7:30.
