Dialogues
Dialogue 1 — A Big Trip
EmmaAre you going to travel during your holiday?
LucasYes! I'm going to spend two weeks in Scotland.
EmmaThat's exciting! Are you going to fly there?
LucasNo, I'm not. I'm going to take the train.
EmmaReally? Why?
LucasBecause I want to see different places on the way.
EmmaHave you planned everything?
LucasAlmost. Yesterday I spoke to a friend who went there last year. He gave me some great ideas.
EmmaWhat did he say?
LucasHe said I should visit Edinburgh and the Highlands.
Dialogue 2 — The Job Interview
AnnaHow did your job interview go?
JohnReally well! I spoke to the manager for almost an hour.
AnnaWere you nervous?
JohnAt first, yes. But then I felt more relaxed.
AnnaDid they ask you many questions?
JohnYes, and I understood most of them.
AnnaDo you think you're going to get the job?
JohnI hope so! They're going to call me on Friday.
AnnaAre you going to leave your current job if they offer it to you?
JohnYes, I am. I'm ready for a change.
Dialogue 3 — Moving Abroad
SophieI heard you're going to move abroad.
DanielYes. I'm going to move to Canada next year.
SophieWow! How do you feel about that?
DanielI'm excited, but I'm also a little scared.
SophieAre you going to live alone?
DanielNo. My girlfriend is going to come with me.
SophieWhat are you going to do there?
DanielI'm going to study, and she's going to look for a job.
SophieWhen did you decide to move?
DanielWe chose Canada a few months ago. Then we began planning everything.

Grammar

Going to — Complete Review

Use be going to + base verb for future plans and intentions.

Affirmative: I'm going to travel next month. / She's going to start a new job. / They're going to move house.

Negative: I'm not going to travel this month. / He isn't going to drive. / They aren't going to stay at a hotel.

Questions: Are you going to travel? / Is she going to study? / Are they going to move?

Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. · Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. · Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Speak → spoke

Present: I speak to my manager every day.
Past: I spoke to my manager yesterday.
Future: I'm going to speak to my manager tomorrow.

Understand → understood

Present: I understand the question.
Past: I understood the question.
Future: I'm going to study more so I can understand everything.

Choose → chose

Present: I usually choose the cheapest option.
Past: We chose Canada.
Future: We're going to choose a hotel tonight.

Run → ran

Present: I run every morning.
Past: I ran five kilometres yesterday.
Future: I'm going to run tomorrow morning.

Break → broke

Present: Be careful! You can break it.
Past: I broke my phone last week.
Future: I'm going to buy a new one because I broke my old one.

Did + Base Verb

She spoke to the manager. → Did she speak to the manager? → She didn't speak to the manager.
They chose Canada. → Did they choose Canada? → They didn't choose Canada.
He broke his phone. → Did he break his phone? → He didn't break his phone.
